Why decay heat matters after a scram

The reactor is already subcritical after a scram. Why do we still care so much about decay heat?

C
Grounded in 3 published passages Ask mode

Because shutting down the chain reaction does not shut down the heat. Fission stops within seconds of the rods going in, but the fission products already in the fuel keep decaying, and that decay deposits energy in the core.

How much: right after a trip from full power, decay heat is roughly 6–7% of the previous thermal power, falling to about 1% within an hour . On a large reactor, 1% is still tens of megawatts, which is why heat removal has to keep working long after the reactor is subcritical .

Following a reactor trip, the heat generated by the decay of fission products must continue to be removed. Although this decay heat is a small fraction of rated power, it is sufficient to damage the fuel if core cooling is lost. For this reason, a path for heat removal must be maintained in every shutdown condition.
